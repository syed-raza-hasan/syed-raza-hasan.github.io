£1.85M of invoiced value never reached clean revenue

A reconciliation of a UK online wholesaler's billing export: 1.04M invoice lines from December 2009 to November 2011. The question was whether the revenue figure could be relied on before any reporting was built on it.

Source UCI Online Retail II (public)Coverage 1,041,845 invoice linesMethod Python, pandas

Key findings

  1. £20.33M was invoiced and £18.49M is clean product revenue. The reconciliation accounts for every pound of the £1.85M difference.
  2. 34,056 duplicate lines account for £495K, 27% of the gap. Postage, fees and normal cancellations explain the remaining 73%.
  3. 628 credit lines refund items the customer had not previously bought, and 13,416 lines were sold at under half the product's usual price.
  4. £192K of Amazon fees sit in the billing file across 34 lines, alongside 1,443 manual adjustments and six bad-debt write-offs totalling £148K. Each needs supporting documentation.

Context

Who uses it
The owner or finance lead, before trusting the revenue figure or closing the year
How often
Once as a look-back over all history, then the same checks every month
Key question
Is revenue clean, and where is money leaking out of billing?
Decision it supports
Which billing steps to fix, which credits to reverse, and which customers to re-bill or refund
Existing report?
Sales reports show totals. None of them flag duplicates, credits with no sale behind them, or under-priced lines.
  1. Metric

    The gap between gross invoiced lines and clean product revenue, split by cause.

  2. Problem

    £20.33M was invoiced. Clean product revenue is £18.49M. Nobody had explained the £1.85M in between.

  3. Hypothesis

    Most of the gap is routine postage and returns, and real leakage is a small slice of it.
    Result: partly supported

  4. Analysis

    Five rules run over every line. Postage, fees and returns explain 73% of the gap. Duplicate lines alone are £495K, the other 27%, and duplicates are never routine.

  5. Experiment

    Check 50 of the under-priced lines against agreed price lists. If most are agreed discounts, drop the rule. If not, add a price-floor check at invoicing.

  6. Decision

    Fix whatever writes duplicate lines first, then require a source invoice or approval for every credit note.

Exception register

Methodology

Duplicates are lines identical in every column: invoice, product, quantity, timestamp, price and customer. Credits with no matching sale are cancellation lines where that customer never bought that product earlier in the file, counted from March 2010 so the first months aren't penalised for sales before the file starts. Under-priced lines are sold below half the product's median price for the same order-size band, only where that product and band has at least 20 sales. Zero-price lines have a quantity but a price of £0. Untraceable sales have no customer ID.

The waterfall uses positive invoice lines as gross, removes exact duplicates, then non-product lines (postage, fees, adjustments, gift vouchers), then product cancellations. What remains is clean product revenue, the same figure the customer analysis starts from.
