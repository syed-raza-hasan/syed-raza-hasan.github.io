US mortgage originations rose 10% in 2025, led by refinancing

Eight years of US mortgage applications, 2018 to 2025, by state, loan purpose and loan type. Select a state on the map or in the table to filter the page.

Source FFIEC HMDA Data BrowserCoverage 120M+ applications, 50 states and DCMethod Python, pandas

Key findings

  1. Originations fell 62% between 2021 and 2023, from 15.1M to 5.7M as rates rose, and recovered to 6.8M in 2025.
  2. Refinance and cash-out loans made up 84% of the 2025 increase. Purchase originations grew 1.6%.
  3. The average originated loan is 37% larger than in 2018, at $353K, and the FHA share of originations rose from 9.2% in 2021 to 13.2%.
  4. Denial rates rose from 12.5% in 2021 to 20.1% in 2023 and eased to 17.8% in 2025.

Context

Who uses it
Head of growth or retail lending at a lender or broker
How often
Quarterly planning, and again when each year's HMDA data lands. Monthly if fed with the lender's own pipeline.
Key question
Which states and loan types should get more loan officers and marketing next year?
Decision it supports
Where to add or cut sales capacity and spend, by state and product
Existing report?
Lenders see their own volume. This adds the whole market, so growth and share have a benchmark.
  1. Metric

    Originations by state and purpose, year on year, with denial rate as the quality check.

  2. Problem

    Volume fell 62% from 2021 to 2023 and is coming back unevenly. Spreading sales effort evenly across states wastes it.

  3. Hypothesis

    The 2025 recovery is refinance-led and strongest in expensive states.
    Result: partly supported

  4. Analysis

    120M applications cut by state, year, purpose and loan type. Refinance and cash-out made up 84% of the 2025 increase, while purchase grew 1.6%. Loan size barely explains which states grew (correlation 0.19).

  5. Experiment

    Move a fixed slice of marketing into the five fastest-growing states for one quarter, keep matched states flat as a control, and compare cost per funded loan.

  6. Decision

    Staff refinance capacity first. Pick expansion states on growth and denial rate together. Loan size is a weak guide.

Methodology

I pulled state-by-year aggregates for 2018 to 2025 from the FFIEC HMDA Data Browser API, two cuts per state and year (action taken by loan purpose, and action taken by loan type), 816 calls in all. Applications are action codes 1 to 5, which leaves out purchased loans and preapproval requests. Originations are code 1, the denial rate is denied applications over all applications, and the average loan is originated dollars over originated count. 2025 is the latest annual release. The 50 states plus DC add up to within 0.3% of the national figure. The gap is Puerto Rico and the other territories, which I left out.

HMDA covers most US mortgage lending but leaves out some small and rural lenders. Data reflects lender submissions and is not adjusted.
