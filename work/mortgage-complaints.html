Insight · Risk and compliance

Mortgage complaints fell 14% while lending fell 62%

189K mortgage complaints sent to the CFPB between January 2019 and August 2026, by company, issue and state, set against each state's loan volume. A service-quality view for compliance and customer teams.

Sources CFPB Consumer Complaint Database, FFIEC HMDAPeriod Jan 2019 to Aug 2026Method Python, pandas

Key findings

  1. Complaint volume stayed between 21K and 27K a year from 2019 to 2025, while originations moved between 15M and 5.7M.
  2. 76% of 2025 complaints concerned paying the mortgage or difficulty paying it, which places the driver in servicing.
  3. There were 36 complaints per 10,000 loans originated in 2025. The District of Columbia was highest at 116 and North Dakota lowest, under 10.
  4. Over the last 12 months one large servicer answered 88.6% of complaints on time. The other 14 companies in the top 15 were at 99% or above.

Context

Who uses it
Head of servicing, complaints or compliance at a mortgage servicer
How often
Weekly for the scorecard, monthly for trends
Key question
Are we drawing more complaints than peers, about what, and are we answering on time?
Decision it supports
Which issue queue gets a process fix or extra staff, and when a response-time slip gets escalated
Existing report?
Raw complaint counts usually exist. Normalising by loan volume and benchmarking against peers is the new part.
  1. Metric

    Complaints per 10K loans, on-time response rate, and share closed with relief.

  2. Problem

    Mortgage complaints held at 21K to 27K a year while originations fell 62%. Raw counts can't tell you whether service is improving.

  3. Hypothesis

    Complaints follow servicing pain, meaning payments and hardship, and move independently of new lending.
    Result: supported

  4. Analysis

    189K complaints by issue, company and state, joined to HMDA loan counts. 76% of 2025 complaints were about paying the mortgage or struggling to pay it.

  5. Experiment

    Send advance notice of payment changes to half of the affected borrowers for 60 days and compare complaints per 10K loans against the other half.

  6. Decision

    Watch per-10K and on-time rate every week. Anything under 95% on time is escalated that week. One large servicer is at 88.6%.

Methodology

The CFPB publishes every complaint it forwards to a company. I streamed the full 5.5 GB file, kept Product = Mortgage, and grouped sub-products into five buckets. Complaints from before 2019 use an older issue list, so the page starts in January 2019. September 2026 is still being filled in and is left out. Relief means the company closed the complaint with monetary or non-monetary relief.

The per-10K ratio divides complaints from a state by HMDA originations in that state for the same year. It is a rough yardstick. Complaints come from everyone with a mortgage, including loans made years ago, so states with older or more distressed loan books will look worse on it.

The CFPB does not verify complaint narratives. Volume reflects how many consumers chose to file and is not a measure of wrongdoing.
