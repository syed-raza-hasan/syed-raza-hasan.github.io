A quarter of a wholesaler's customers generate 70% of its revenue

Two years of invoices, December 2009 to November 2011, from a UK online wholesaler that sells mainly to small retailers: where revenue comes from, which customers have lapsed, and which products carry the range.

Source UCI Online Retail II (public)Coverage 1.07M invoice linesMethod Python, pandas

Key findings

  1. 1,445 customers, a quarter of those identified, generated 70% of revenue. The median one had placed 10 orders and last ordered 15 days before the cut-off.
  2. 841 previously regular customers had not ordered in about a year. Their past spend totals £1.5M, which makes them the first list for a win-back programme.
  3. September to November 2011 produced 38% of the year's sales. Stock and staffing plans for the peak need to start by mid-year.
  4. 1,050 of 4,893 products, 21% of the range, produced 80% of sales.

Context

Who uses it
The owner and sales lead of a small wholesaler
How often
Monthly, before the sales team plans its calls
Key question
Which customers should we call this month, and which products must never run out?
Decision it supports
The monthly call list and pre-season stock orders
Existing report?
Most wholesalers have monthly sales totals. Customer segments and cohorts are new.
  1. Metric

    Revenue by customer segment, cohort repeat rate, and revenue concentration by product.

  2. Problem

    70% of revenue sits with a quarter of named customers, and 841 customers who used to order regularly have gone quiet.

  3. Hypothesis

    Growth comes from existing customers buying more, so retention is the lever.
    Result: supported

  4. Analysis

    1M invoice lines turned into cohorts and recency and frequency segments. In November 2011 returning customers brought in £1.06M and new customers £80K. 1,050 of 4,893 products make 80% of sales.

  5. Experiment

    Call half of the at-risk list with a reorder offer, leave the other half alone, and compare reorder rates after 60 days.

  6. Decision

    Run a monthly win-back list from the at-risk segment, and lock stock for the top 1,050 products before September.

Methodology

The raw file has 1.07M invoice lines across two Excel sheets with about 34K exact duplicates, which I dropped. I took out postage, bank charges, manual adjustments and other non-product codes, and lines with a zero price. One 74,215-unit storage jar order was cancelled 16 minutes after it was placed, so I removed both halves of it. Invoice numbers starting with C are cancellations and count as returns. December 2011 stops on the 9th, so the trend ends in November. Cohort and segment views only use the 77% of lines that carry a customer ID.

Segments use quintile scores for recency (days since last order) and frequency (number of invoices). Champions score 4 or 5 on both. At risk means 3+ on frequency but 1 or 2 on recency.
